Keller as the delivered ELDT course while adding deliberate instruction in hours-of-service calculations, paper/electronic logging, a road atlas, route/trip planning, and practical dispatch scenarios. This does **not** revise the existing four following program weeks, behind-the-wheel hours or the Academy OS 160-hour tracking model.

**Implementation location:** Training → Take Attendance → **Lesson Plans** (the existing Week-one planner). The blue/silver Week 1 classroom panel is available without adding more sidebar navigation. Instructors/management can see five proposed day guides, all 30 federally required Class A standard theory topics, practical workshop plans and evidence checklists. On campus selection, an authorized staff member may **Add missing days to Lesson Plans**. ## Non-negotiable compliance boundaries
- 49 CFR Part 380, Appendix A requires coverage of the **30 standard Class A theory units** in five sections: basic operation (7), safe operating procedures (7), advanced operating practices (3), vehicle systems and reporting malfunctions (3), and non-driving activities (10). **Do not** mistake an assignment in the planner for instruction delivered or an FMCSA training certification.
- Federal theory specifies **no fixed minimum training hours**; the school must cover the relevant curriculum and trainees must achieve **at least 80%** on their theory assessment. The standard Class A curriculum may differ from the separate Class B-to-A upgrade curriculum.
- For its standard course, North Carolina rule **19A NCAC 03J .0306** specifies **50 classroom instructional hours including testing** (as part of its broader 160-hour framework). Our five days are an initial **10 net instructional hours per day** proposal; **breaks/lunch do not count**. This is not an approved campus schedule. The school must check that every assigned licensed J. J. Keller unit really fits within this time and extend/rebalance instruction if it doesn't.
- Lone Star school orientation takes the first **six proposed hours Monday**. Staff must verify that the required federal orientation content is covered within or in addition to this school-specific session.
- Required vendor theory, documented classroom attendance, independent school practicals, paper/ELD worksheets, pass/fail assessments, remediation and FMCSA TPR confirmation are **different records**, not interchangeable substitutes.
- This feature **does not** sign off federal theory, mark modules complete, change student hours, submit to the Training Provider Registry or certify regulatory compliance. Use existing Academy OS attendance, assessments and TPR tracking separately.

## Monday — orientation and basic operation
- 6-hour school orientation: policy, safety, CDL responsibilities, regulations, classroom expectations, training sequence and employment terminology.
- Federal theory `A1.1.1–A1.1.7`: Orientation, dashboard/controls, pre/post/enroute inspections, basic vehicle control, shifting, backing/docking and coupling.
- Use J. J. Keller materials and combination-vehicle diagrams.
- Evidence: school orientation acknowledgment, verified course/module completions, knowledge check.

## Tuesday — safe operation & emergency decision-making
- Federal theory `A1.2.1–A1.2.7`: visual search, communication, distraction, speed, space, night operation, extreme conditions.
- Federal theory `A1.3.1–A1.3.3`: hazard perception, skid/recovery/jackknifing and emergencies, railroad-highway crossings.
- Include recorded hazard scenarios and discussion of unsafe route decisions; this is *theory*, not a substitute for range or road proficiency.
- Evidence: J. J. Keller results, hazard recognition work, corrections after an instructor review.

## Wednesday — vehicle systems + hours of service, paper logs and ELD
- Federal theory `A1.4.1–A1.4.3`: malfunctions, roadside inspections, maintenance.
- Federal theory `A1.5.3–A1.5.4`: HOS and fatigue/wellness.
- **Practical lesson:** the 11-hour driving limit, the ordinary 14-hour window, the 30-minute qualifying break after eight cumulative driving hours without one, 60/70-hour cycles, 34-hour restart, and applicability of exceptions.
- Student worksheet A: A driver begins on-duty at **06:00**, performs a **30-minute pre-trip**, drives starting at **06:30**, and spends **two hours at a shipper**. Under the ordinary property-carrying rule, the 14-hour driving window ordinarily ends **20:00**. The shipper delay does *not* automatically extend the window. Students must calculate remaining driving, break eligibility and legal next steps; explicitly discuss any exceptions only when the scenario warrants them.
- Student worksheet B: complete a paper 24-hour grid using off duty, sleeper berth, driving and on-duty-not-driving; reconcile **24 hours** total.
- Student worksheet C: calculate the 60/70-hour cycle using a supplied 7/8-day log recap, explain a qualifying restart and avoid double counting.
- Student worksheet D: navigate a **demo ELD**, annotate/correct records, explain duty status and identify malfunctions. Do not use a production ELD to fabricate driving entries.
- Evidence: independent calculations, completed logs and recap, practical check, reteaching note for critical mistakes. This classroom practice does not satisfy A3.7 BTW-road proficiency.

## Thursday — cargo, atlas & trip planning
- Federal theory `A1.5.1–A1.5.2`: cargo handling, documentation and environmental compliance.
- Federal theory `A1.5.8`: safe trip planning, route restrictions, rail clearance, rest/parking, vehicle limits, permits and GPS limitations.
- **Atlas lab:** identify interstates, state lines, distance charts, bridge/height/weight restrictions, designated truck routes and safe truck parking.
- **Fictional dispatch:** Greensboro, NC → Dallas, TX, 53-foot dry van. Supply a *fictional* pickup/delivery window, tractor/trailer specifications, initial HOS clock and shipment document. Students must verify legal truck routes and current road restrictions, calculate realistic ETA with time-zone change, locate legal fuel/rest/parking, and explain whether the delivery window is achievable. **Do not assume mileage or use this simulated route as an actual dispatch authorization.**
- Mid-exercise, change the delivery appointment and require a written response to dispatch if the revised request cannot legally be met.
- Evidence: signed route-selection worksheet, HOS and ETA calculation, map markings and written driver-dispatch communication.

## Friday — remaining required topics, assessment, driver readiness
- Federal theory `A1.5.5–A1.5.7`: post-crash, external communications, whistleblower/coercion.
- Federal theory `A1.5.9–A1.5.10`: drug/alcohol and medical requirements.
- Reserve structured time for completing any required J. J. Keller modules and remediation.
- **Lone Star Professional Driver Challenge:** give every trainee a fictional load confirmation, truck specifications, an ELD or paper-log practice sheet, an atlas and a delivery time. Have them produce a legal trip plan, identify HOS restrictions and write a dispatcher update.
- Add a brief first-job pay terminology segment: per mile, hourly, percentage, detention and layover. This is supplemental information, not a separately mandated federal theory unit.
- Instructor records a school practical rubric and any remediation needs. A proposed school 80% threshold is **supplemental** and not a substitute for the federally required theory assessment.
